## The decision this week is built around

`plan.md`'s original Week 7 was shakeouts-only anyway — this week was always going to be light. What's changed is *why* it's light: illness, not taper discipline. Fever/chest symptoms were involved, and Josh isn't confirmed clearly past the standard 24-48h fever-free window as of Tue Sep 22. That rule outranks the training plan. Running through an active or recently-resolved fever risks real cardiac strain (viral myocarditis), not just a bad session — this is a harder line than the joint-pain rule, not a softer one.

**This week has one job: get Josh to the start line healthy.** Fitness is not going to change in 5 days. Pushing a test run before the body is clearly ready can turn "missed a taper week" into "can't start the race at all."

## Non-negotiable this week

1. **No running today (Tue) without a clear 24-48h fever-free confirmation.** This is the same category as the joint-pain rule but stricter, because the downside (cardiac strain) is worse than a knee setback.
2. **Any test run stops immediately at chest tightness, dizziness, or HR that feels wrong for the effort** — full stop, not "push through to finish the mile."
3. **If Wed/Thu don't happen at all, that's fine.** Zero running this week and going into the race on rest is a real, safe option — it will not fix itself into good fitness, but it won't hurt you either. The only bad outcome this week is running before the body's actually ready.

## How to run the race itself

Be direct about where this leaves the goals from the dossier (`races/2026-08-10-ealing-half-marathon-dossier.md`):

- **A (sub-1:40) and B (1:42) are off the table for this race.** Not because the fitness disappeared, but because there's been ~2.5mi of running in the last 12 days, training status reads DETRAINING, and there's been zero race-pace calibration since Aug 29. Chasing 7:47/mi off this base is exactly the "going out too hot" failure mode the whole plan was built to avoid — now amplified by a body that's just fought off an illness.
- **Goal C — finish injury/illness-free, well-paced — is now the only goal, and that's fine.** The dossier already said this outranks time when the two conflict.
- **Run it by feel and HR, not pace.** Start conservatively (low Z2, 130s bpm) for the first 2-3 miles regardless of what the crowd/pacers around you are doing — more true this week than ever, since there's no fitness reserve to bail you out of a hot start. If it feels genuinely easy by mile 5-6, you can let it drift up slightly, but keep it controlled.
- **Walk-run is a legitimate strategy, not a failure**, especially past mile 8-10 if energy dips — treat aid stations as walk breaks by default rather than something to fight through.
- **Any chest tightness, dizziness, or unusual breathlessness during the race = stop and walk, or step off if it doesn't resolve.** This isn't the mile 10-11 crowd-pacing fade the dossier warns about — that's a pacing problem; this is a health check, and it overrides everything else including finishing.
- Expect a slower day than the ~1:51 honest projection from a month ago, given the detraining and the illness — that's the correct outcome given what the last two weeks actually were, not a sign anything went wrong in the buildup itself.
