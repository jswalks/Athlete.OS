## Non-negotiable this week

1. **No running today (Tue) without a clear 24-48h fever-free confirmation.** This is the same category as the joint-pain rule but stricter, because the downside (cardiac strain) is worse than a knee setback.

3. **If Wed/Thu don't happen at all, that's fine.** Zero running this week and going into the race on rest is a real, safe option — it will not fix itself into good fitness, but it won't hurt you either. The only bad outcome this week is running before the body's actually ready.

## How to run the race itself

Be direct about where this leaves the goals from the dossier (`races/2026-08-10-ealing-half-marathon-dossier.md`):

- **A (sub-1:40) and B (1:42) are off the table for this race.** Not because the fitness disappeared, but because there's been ~2.5mi of running in the last 12 days, training status reads DETRAINING, and there's been zero race-pace calibration since Aug 29. - **Goal C — finish injury/illness-free, well-paced — is now the only goal, and that's fine.** The dossier already said this outranks time when the two conflict.
- **Run it by feel and HR, not pace.** Start conservatively (low Z2, 130s bpm) for the first 2-3 miles regardless of what the crowd/pacers around you are doing — more true this week than ever, since there's no fitness reserve to bail you out of a hot start. - **Any chest tightness, dizziness, or unusual breathlessness during the race = stop and walk, or step off if it doesn't resolve.** This isn't the mile 10-11 crowd-pacing fade the dossier warns about — that's a pacing problem; this is a health check, and it overrides everything else including finishing.

Every objective marker points away from active illness: respiration rate flat at 13–14, RHR back to 43, HRV fully recovered at 71/72 BALANCED, and Wednesday's run produced a completely normal HR-for-effort response. That is a reassuring picture and it argues the infection has cleared.

**Why this still gets you a safe, strong race, not just a preferred one:**

Running steady and genuinely easy for the first half does most of the same job, provided "steady" actually means Z1/low-Z2 (130-140bpm), not settling into whatever pace feels normal.

Choosing to walk there is not a failure; not needing to is a good sign.
- **One line that doesn't move regardless of pacing style: any chest tightness, pressure, or breathlessness out of proportion to effort during the race is a stop-and-walk, not "push to the next mile."** Raspy is cleared; anything past raspy isn't.

**Race-day shape:** controlled, low-effort opening 3-4 miles (HR 130-140, whatever pace that is), then let effort rise as it genuinely feels sustainable rather than fading late — the "consistent strong race" Josh is after is an even or negative-split effort curve, which is also the single best defence against the mile 10-11 fade the dossier already flagged as this course's main risk. Realistic finish still ~2:05-2:20 given the training gap; the time is incidental to finishing controlled and healthy.
